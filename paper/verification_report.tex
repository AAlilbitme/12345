\begin{abstract}
We formalise an abstraction of Lightning Network multi-hop HTLC (Hash
Time-Locked Contract) routing in the Tamarin symbolic prover under a
Dolev--Yao network adversary. The artefact is a \emph{modular package of five
theories} totalling \textbf{58 machine-checked lemmas}. It covers a two-party
channel lifecycle, fixed three-hop signed routing, relational and block-level
timing abstractions, and finite-clock witnesses. The principal guarantees are
\emph{conditional}: payment atomicity and intermediary safety rely on explicit
online-action assumptions, while conservation concerns symbolic routed HTLC
amounts rather than the balances of Bitcoin commitment outputs. Within this
scope, we (i) replace a global forwarding-replay axiom with a structural
linear-resource guard;
(ii) machine-check the known \emph{wormhole} fee-theft attack, both as a
reachability result and as an equality between the skipped amount and the
symbolic path-fee difference; (iii) prove routed-amount equations and expose a
griefing DoS; and (iv) demonstrate empirically that the intermediary-claim
assumption T3 is load-bearing.
A recurring theme is \emph{tractability}: in the tested configuration, signing
$+$ natural-number induction $+$ an unbounded block clock exhausts the prover
under our resource bounds. We also map two further empirical
boundaries---signed-message $N$-hop routing and reusable channels---where
automated search stops scaling.
All reported prover verdicts and bounded timeouts were executed under the
commands and limits stated in the artefact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}

Lightning is a Layer-2 payment-channel network: parties transact off-chain and
use the blockchain only to settle disputes. A multi-hop payment routes value
along a path $\text{Alice}\!\to\!\text{Bob}\!\to\!\text{Carol}\!\to\!\text{Dave}$
by chaining HTLCs that share one payment hash $y=H(x)$; the receiver reveals
the preimage $x$, which propagates back along the path, redeeming each HTLC.
Our reference model is the standard textbook presentation
\cite{maffei-slides}: a four-party path, per-hop fees, staggered CLTV timelocks
$3t/2t/t$, and a two-round ``lock then release'' commit. The Lightning
specification itself is maintained as the BOLT documents \cite{bolts}.

\paragraph{Threat model.} The adversary controls the symbolic network: it may
intercept, replay, synthesize, and reroute any derivable message, and may reveal
long-term signing keys through explicit compromise rules. Cryptographic
primitives are perfect modulo Tamarin's equational theories. The adversary does
not break hashes or signatures, control Bitcoin consensus, or violate the
trace restrictions stated below.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.} We model integrity and trace safety, not
payment privacy or unlinkability (which require onion routing and
observational-equivalence proofs). The five theories are separate labelled
transition systems: their results support one another conceptually, but no
formal refinement theorem transfers a lemma from one theory to another. In the
shipped fixed-hop model, the linear \texttt{Free(ptr)} guard is consumed and
not returned, so a channel hosts one HTLC over its modelled lifetime. HTLC
amounts are related by symbolic natural-number equations but are not debited
from the \texttt{StateA}/\texttt{StateB} channel balances; ``value
conservation'' below therefore means routed-amount conservation, not ledger
solvency. Timing is relational in the main theory, while separate theories
show that numerical CLTV claim windows exist. They do not prove that an online
party acts; T2b/T3 state that liveness boundary explicitly.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Within those limits, the artefact contributes:
(1) a signed fixed-hop Dolev--Yao model with an explicit assumption matrix;
(2) structural replay hardening using a linear resource rather than a global
single-spend axiom; (3) machine-checked reachability of known wormhole and
griefing behaviours; (4) an idealised linear-fact generalisation of routing
safety to arbitrary path length; and (5) reproducible positive and negative
results characterising where Tamarin's automated search ceases to scale.

\paragraph{Artefact.} Sources, figures, and the runner are available at
\url{https://github.com/AAlilbitme/12345}. The core Stage~1 model corresponds
to commit \texttt{d78740a}; the reusable-channel experiment to
\texttt{2700974}. Exact commands appear in Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}.

\section{Architecture: a modular five-theory package}
\label{sec:arch}

A single monolithic theory is intractable: combining Tamarin's
signing equational theory, natural-number induction, and an unbounded block
clock exhausted memory in the tested configuration. Pairwise experimental
variants remained tractable under the recorded bounds. This is empirical tool
evidence, not a general complexity claim. The model is therefore split so that
no single core theory carries all three (Table~\ref{tab:files}).

\section{Related work}

Tamarin performs symbolic trace reasoning over multiset-rewriting rules and
equational theories \cite{tamarin-csf,tamarin-cav}. Boyd, Gjøsteen, and Wu
model a blockchain and an HTLC-based cross-chain protocol in Tamarin
\cite{boyd-htlc}; their ledger and global-time treatment is richer than the
relational timing abstraction in our main theory. Hüttel and Staroveški use
ProVerif to study secrecy and authenticity of four Lightning subprotocols
\cite{huttel-staroveski}. Grundmann and Hartenstein model check Lightning in
TLA+ using explicit refinement arguments to separate channel and multi-hop
reasoning \cite{grundmann-hartenstein}; in contrast, our modular split is a
tractability device without a machine-checked refinement between theories.
Malavolta et al.\ identify the wormhole attack and propose anonymous multi-hop
locks \cite{amhl}; our contribution is not discovery of that attack, but a
Tamarin witness and symbolic fee-difference characterization in this model.
The original Lightning proposal and the BOLT specifications provide the
protocol context \cite{poon-dryja,bolts}.

\section{Methodology}

All results were produced under a strict \emph{test-first} discipline: a
property is claimed only after Tamarin reports it, and any ``plausible''
existence result is re-checked with a tightened variant before being trusted
(Section~\ref{sec:falsepos} shows this catching a false positive). Restrictions
(global trace filters) are treated as \emph{assumptions}, not proofs; a
suspiciously short proof is a warning sign. Arithmetic theories motivate the
timing orderings, while online action remains an explicit liveness assumption.
Likewise, a timeout is a measured negative tool outcome under a stated command
and wall-clock bound, not a theorem that no proof or witness exists.

Per-lemma invocation (rather than one bulk \texttt{--prove}) is used on the
large theory to avoid OOM:
\begin{lstlisting}
LANG=C.utf8 LC_ALL=C.utf8 tamarin-prover multihop.spthy \
  --heuristic=c --stop-on-trace=seqdfs --derivcheck-timeout=0 --prove=<Lemma>
\end{lstlisting}
The flag \texttt{--stop-on-trace=seqdfs} rescues the heavy existence witnesses
but \emph{hangs} the natural-number arithmetic theories, so the runner applies
it per file and lemma. The whole suite is driven by \texttt{run.py}; the
\texttt{Makefile} is a thin wrapper.

\subsection{Timing safety (\texttt{cltv_blocks}, \texttt{gaps}, \texttt{t2b_attack})}

Figure~\ref{fig:timing} shows the staggered-timelock story.
\texttt{cltv_blocks} proves arithmetic consequences of explicit deadline
restrictions---in particular that the assumed stagger gives a non-empty claim
window. \texttt{gaps} proves lifecycle properties in a \emph{different},
linear-channel/block-clock abstraction (including no HTLC addition after close)
and provides redeem/refund witnesses. These lemmas do not automatically hold in
\texttt{multihop.spthy}. Finally, \texttt{t2b_attack} is a minimal
over-approximating theory that exhibits the early-timeout ordering possible
without T2b; it is not literally the main theory with one restriction deleted.

\section{Security analysis}

\subsection{Forwarding-replay hardening (axiom to structure)}

The forwarding rules read the outgoing amount/fee from the network and are
gated only by a public HTLC-offer message. A Dolev--Yao replay of that offer
could re-fire an honest forward, locking a \emph{second} HTLC on the same
channel output with an adversary-chosen amount. This was found empirically via
an audit probe (double-forward: reachable) and initially blocked with a global
axiom \texttt{OneHTLCPerPtr}. We then replaced the axiom with a \emph{structural}
guard: each channel open emits one linear \texttt{Free(ptr)} token, and every
HTLC-add consumes it. ``At most one HTLC per output'' is now a consequence of
linear-fact consumption---one fewer assumption for the same guarantee. After
the change the double-forward probe is falsified structurally (100 steps, no
axiom) and all 43 lemmas still verify. We also confirmed that a symmetric
``double-redeem'' is \emph{not} a vulnerability: the redeem/settle rules each
consume a linear pending fact, so replay cannot double-spend an output.

\subsection{Wormhole attack (reachable and quantified)}

The wormhole attack (Malavolta et al., NDSS'19): two colluding path members
relay the preimage out-of-band, skipping an honest intermediary who forwards
value but earns no fee \cite{amhl}. In the symbolic model the public
Dolev--Yao network over-approximates such a side channel; collusion and final
account ownership are not represented explicitly. We machine-check it twice:
\texttt{Wormhole_Fee_Theft_Reachable} (51 steps) exhibits a trace where the
payment settles yet the middle hop is never redeemed and no intermediary earns
a fee, with \emph{no} key compromise; and
\texttt{Wormhole_Steals_Exactly_The_Fees} (81 steps) shows that the symbolic
sender/receiver amount difference equals $\%f_1 \%{+}\,\%f_2$ while neither
intermediary emits \texttt{FeeEarned}. Because channel balances and transfers
to colluders are not modeled, this is a skipped-fee characterization rather
than a ledger-level proof of who receives stolen coins. Anonymous multi-hop
locks address the known attack class \cite{amhl}.

\subsection{Economic soundness and griefing}

The routed-amount lemmas are direct consequences of equations emitted by the
forward rules. \texttt{EndToEnd_Value_Conservation} composes the two per-hop
equations, but does not constrain the separate channel-state balances.
\texttt{Fee_Strictly_Positive_Hop1/2} follows because this encoding uses
Tamarin's positive-natural sort (there is no $\%0$); it therefore records a
modeling choice and should not be read as a claim that Lightning forbids
zero-fee channels. As a reachability result,
\texttt{Griefing_Capital_Lock_Reachable} shows a trace labeled as capital
lock/refund with no completed payment, abstracting the standard PCN griefing
DoS.

\subsection{Assumption minimality (T3 is load-bearing)}
\label{sec:t3}

A natural question is whether the intermediary-claim restriction T3 is redundant
given T1 (redeem-before-timeout) and T2b (honest parties act before the inbound
timeout). We tested it by removing T3 and re-running the suite: the safety lemma
\texttt{Intermediary_Never_Loses_Under_Liveness} is then \emph{falsified},
Tamarin finding a concrete 59-step counterexample. T2b orders the two timeouts
but does not force the node to \emph{sweep} the incoming HTLC once its outgoing
one is redeemed; that action is exactly what T3 encodes. This establishes that
T3 is load-bearing relative to the remaining assumptions. It does \emph{not}
establish independent minimality of every timing restriction; doing so would
require one removal experiment and reproducible counterexample per restriction.

\section{Generalisation to N hops}

The core model fixes the path at three hops. Replacing the two hardcoded forward
rules with one \emph{generic} rule was attempted in several encodings, all run:

Linear-fact routing (idealised channel) & safety invariants verify by induction over the chain, any $N$ & \gc \\

The linear-fact route yields \texttt{multihop_nhop.spthy} (28/29 lemmas; the one
open lemma, \texttt{Settle_Requires_Receiver_Release}, keys on the terminal
settle and does not terminate) and, with amounts/fees added,
\texttt{multihop_nhop_fees.spthy} (31/31, verified in 2\,min\,02\,s on the
test machine). The latter signs the invoice but represents forwarding and
fulfilment by internal linear facts rather than network messages. It is
therefore an idealised routing-state abstraction, not a cryptographic
generalisation of the fixed-hop model. The two layers have no formal refinement
proof.

\section{What failed, and the tractability boundaries}
\label{sec:failed}

We record negative results explicitly---they are the paper's methodological
contribution as much as the positive ones.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The three-ingredient OOM.} Signing $+$ natural-number induction
  $+$ an unbounded block clock together exhausted the tested configuration.
  Selected pairwise variants remained tractable; the artefact should be read as
  empirical evidence under the stated resource limits, not a general
  complexity theorem. This motivates the modular split
  (Section~\ref{sec:arch}).

\item \textbf{Signed-message $N$-hop routing} (Table~\ref{tab:nhop}): origin
  analysis on the self-referential \texttt{'htlc'} term does not terminate,
  even for reachability, even with a fresh id.

\item \textbf{A rejected restriction.} An initial fix \texttt{OneRedeemPerPtr}
  (``at most one redeem per output'') was tested and \emph{rejected}: it
  falsified the honest witness \texttt{Multihop_Payment_Possible}, because the
  two \texttt{Redeemed} events on the sender's output in an honest run are the
  two endpoints' views of one hop, not two spends. The correct guard was
  \texttt{OneHTLCPerPtr} (later made structural).

\item \textbf{A caught false positive.} \label{sec:falsepos}
  An exists-trace probe that shared only the payment \emph{hash} between an
  offer and a payment appeared to show ``value creation'' (receiver paid more
  than the sender locked, no compromise). Tightening it to a single
  \emph{linked} payment chain \emph{falsified} it, while a control confirmed the
  chain is otherwise reachable---so the apparent attack was invoice-hash reuse
  across two unrelated offers, not a soundness bug. This is the
  adversarial-verification discipline in action.

\item \textbf{Reusable channels (boundary).} We staged a refactor toward
  faithful reusable channels: Stage~1 (structural single-HTLC-per-output via the
  \texttt{Free(ptr)} slot) ships and re-verifies 43/43. Stage~2 (return
  \texttt{Free(ptr)} on resolution, so a channel carries sequential HTLCs) was
  spiked and is a clean tractability boundary: without \texttt{--auto-sources}
  every lemma returns ``analysis incomplete'' (the reuse loop leaves unbounded
  partial deconstructions); \emph{with} \texttt{--auto-sources} the
  backward-reasoning safety lemmas verify ($\sim$45\,s each), but every
  exists-trace witness and the interval lemma
  \texttt{No_Concurrent_HTLC_Per_Output} fail to converge. The original
  interval formula was unguarded; after splitting redeem/refund into two
  guarded existential disjuncts, the theory passes
  \texttt{--quit-on-warning}, but the corrected lemma still produced no result
  after approximately 786\,s. A tightly constrained sender-only reuse witness
  timed out at 900\,s under both default and SEQDFS search.
  Reusable channels are expressible and their core safety survives, but the loop
  reintroduces the witness/liveness non-termination the modular split avoids;
  Stage~1 is therefore the shipped model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:reproduction}

\begin{lstlisting}
export LANG=C.utf8 LC_ALL=C.utf8
python3 run.py                 # full core suite (5 theories)
python3 run.py multihop.spthy  # one theory
make nhop                      # N-hop fee abstraction (31/31)
\end{lstlisting}
The recorded core results use Tamarin 1.8 and Maude 3.1. The N-hop-fee suite
was additionally re-run with Tamarin 1.12.0 and Maude 3.5 (31/31); step counts
may differ across versions. The Makefile delegates to \texttt{run.py}, which
selects per-file strategies and returns nonzero if a lemma is falsified,
incomplete, missing, or timed out. The Stage~2 experiment requires
\texttt{--auto-sources}; its timeout observations use the explicit limits
reported above.

\section{Conclusion}

We delivered a modular, machine-checked \emph{abstraction} of Lightning
multi-hop HTLC routing (58 lemmas over five separate theories). The fixed-hop
model replaces a replay-prevention axiom with a linear structural guard,
reproduces known wormhole and griefing behaviours, and verifies authentication,
causality, and routed-amount equations under explicit assumptions. Separate
theories establish arithmetic claim windows, post-close exclusion in a linear
channel abstraction, and finite-clock witnesses. These results do not constitute
a unified balance-correctness proof of the BOLT protocol: channel-balance
integration, formal refinement between theories, privacy, concurrent HTLCs, and
unbounded reusable channels remain open. The durable contribution is
methodological and empirical---a reproducible account of which abstractions keep
Tamarin tractable, which assumptions carry the conditional guarantees, and
where signed generic routing and reusable-state witness search stop scaling.
