\section{Bypass Resistance for Routes of Any Length}\label{sec:bypass}

The wormhole of Sec.~\ref{sec:wormhole} lets a payment settle while an honest forwarder between
two compromised parties is bypassed: its outgoing HTLC is refunded and it earns no fee. We now
show that in our model a bypass requires key compromise: for the forwarder at each of the
positions 1 to 6 of a route (under T1 to T3), and, for payments whose hash is offered on a single channel, for a
forwarder at any position of a route of any length. Secs.~\ref{sec:bypass-fixed}
to~\ref{sec:bypass-caveats} concern the main theory \code{lightning.spthy}; there is no separate
N-hop theory. HTLC offers between channel peers are not network messages but the private,
single-use fact \code{Route}, which each application of the one generic rule
\code{Forward_HTLC} consumes and re-creates for the next hop (Sec.~\ref{sec:core-routing}): with
the offer as a public, signed message, \tamarin{} treats every party as a potential sender when
tracing a forwarded message, and the analysis of routes of unbounded length did not terminate
(what the adversary loses: Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Bypass Requires Compromise}\label{sec:bypass-fixed}

The only rule that produces a \code{Fulfilled} fact for an HTLC that was not redeemed is
\code{AttackerSkip} (Sec.~\ref{sec:core-skip}), which requires the parties at both ends of the
skip to be compromised; \code{Fulfilled_Source} states that every \code{Fulfilled} fact used in
a settlement stems from an earlier redemption or skip, and every skip follows a compromise
(Lemma~\ref{lem:skip}). A forwarder is \emph{bypassed} when the payment settles at the sender while the forwarder's own
hop is refunded; \code{Intermediary_Timeout_Refund} records such a refund as
\code{RefundHop(ptrIn,ptrOut,y)}. For the forwarders at the first six positions of the sender's
route we prove, in the full model with T1, T2 and T3 in place:

\begin{theorem}[Bypass Resistance at Positions 1 to 6]
Let $1 \le k \le 6$ and assume T1 to T3. If a sender settles a payment with preimage $x$ on its own channel, the
HTLC on $h(x)$ is forwarded hop by hop from that channel by forwarders $F_1, \dots, F_k$, and
the hop of $F_k$ is refunded, then some party's long-term key was compromised.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Its six instances, \code{First_Hop_Skip_Requires_Compromise} to
\code{Hop6_Skip_Requires_Compromise} (Appendix~\ref{app:bypass}), spell out the route:
\code{SenderSettled} on the sender's channel \code{pSF}, a chain of $k$ \code{HTLCForwarded}
facts on $h(x)$ in which $F_1$ forwards the HTLC on \code{pSF} and each $F_{i+1}$ the outgoing
HTLC of $F_i$, and \code{RefundHop} for the hop of $F_k$. With their seven helper lemmas (group 9
of Table~\ref{tab:groups}), they are the 13 hop-bypass lemmas of \texttt{make verify}.

The wormhole trace \code{Wormhole_Fee_Theft_Reachable} (Fig.~\ref{fig:wormhole}), with Indiana
as the second forwarder ($F_1$ is Att1, $F_2$ is Indiana), satisfies the premise of
\code{Second_Hop_Skip_Requires_Compromise}; the compromise in the conclusion is that of Att1 and
Att2, which \code{AttackerSkip} requires, so the conclusion cannot be dropped.

\subsection{Routes of Any Length}\label{sec:bypass-any}

Each fixed-position theorem names the route up to the bypassed forwarder and therefore covers
one position only; the general theorem quantifies over any forward of the payment hash instead.

\begin{theorem}[Bypass Resistance at Any Position]\label{thm:anyhop}
If a sender settles a payment with preimage $x$ on its channel $p_{SF}$, every HTLC on $h(x)$
is offered on $p_{SF}$, and some forwarder of an HTLC on $h(x)$, at any position, has its hop
refunded, then some party's long-term key was compromised.
\end{theorem}
\begin{spthy}
lemma Any_Hop_Skip_Requires_Compromise [heuristic={lemmas_first}]:
  all-traces
  "∀ S F1 R pSF x Me Prev Next pIn pOut #s #f #r.
    ((((SenderSettled( S, F1, R, pSF, x ) @ #s) ∧
       (HTLCForwarded( Me, Prev, Next, pIn, pOut, h(x) ) @ #f)) ∧
      (RefundHop( pIn, pOut, h(x) ) @ #r)) ∧
     (∀ A B p #o. (HTLCOffered( A, B, p, h(x) ) @ #o) ⇒ (p = pSF))) ⇒
    (∃ P #c. LtkCompromised( P ) @ #c)"
\end{spthy}
The last conjunct of the premise, every offer for $h(x)$ is on \code{pSF}, is the
\emph{single-offer premise}. No position appears in the statement: \code{Me} is any forwarder of
$h(x)$.

\subsection{Proof}\label{sec:bypass-proof}

The proof rests on seven helper lemmas, proved in the same run.
\code{Forward_Parent}: the incoming HTLC of every forward was offered by a sender or forwarded
by the previous hop. \code{Root_First}: every forward of a payment hash is, or is preceded by, a
forward whose incoming HTLC was offered by a sender. \code{Next_Is_Child}: under the
single-offer premise, if \code{Prev} forwarded an HTLC to \code{Me}, every later forward of the
hash is \code{Me}'s forward of that HTLC or comes after it. \code{Hop_Redeem_Propagates} and
\code{Sender_Channel_Redeemed}: without compromise, if a forwarder's incoming HTLC is redeemed,
so is its outgoing one, and if the sender settles, its own channel was redeemed.
\code{Route_Redeemed_Gen}: without compromise and under the single-offer premise, redemption of
the incoming HTLC of a forward carries over to the incoming HTLC of every forward of the hash
that does not come earlier. \code{Route_Redeemed_No_Compromise} combines it with
\code{Sender_Channel_Redeemed} and \code{Root_First}: without compromise and under the
single-offer premise, if the sender settles, the incoming HTLC of every forward of the hash is
redeemed. \code{Root_First}, \code{Next_Is_Child} and \code{Route_Redeemed_Gen} are proved by
induction over traces; their proof scripts, and those of \code{Route_Redeemed_No_Compromise} and
of the theorem, are stored in \code{lightning.spthy}, and the other helpers are proved
automatically.

The theorem then takes 7 proof steps. Assume no compromise. By
\code{Route_Redeemed_No_Compromise}, the bypassed forwarder's incoming HTLC \code{pIn} is
redeemed. By \code{Redeemed_Source}, this redemption is either the settlement of a forward from
\code{pIn}, which \code{Forward_Unique_Per_Incoming}, \code{SettledHop_From_Forward} and
\code{No_Settle_And_Refund_Same_Hop} exclude given \code{RefundHop(pIn,pOut,h(x))}, or a
fulfilment by the receiver, which \code{No_Fulfill_On_Forwarded_Incoming} excludes.

\paragraph{General Mode and Trace Inclusion}
The theorem is proved in a separate mode of the same theory, \texttt{make general} (flags in
Appendix~\ref{app:catalogue}), which enables the general lemmas and removes T1, T2 and T3. Removing a restriction only adds
traces, so an all-traces property proved in this mode also holds in the full model. The run
proves the theorem together with all 23 \code{[reuse]} lemmas available in this mode (24/24
verified), so no lemma proved under the removed restrictions is reused (\code{ForwardTimeGap},
derived from T2, is not a \code{[reuse]} lemma in this mode). Bypass resistance for
single-offer payments therefore does not depend on T1, T2 or T3.

\subsection{Caveats}\label{sec:bypass-caveats}

\emph{Single-offer premise and liveness restrictions.} Theorem~\ref{thm:anyhop} is proved only
for payments whose hash is offered on a single channel; if the same hash is offered on several
channels, several routes carry it, and the theorem says nothing about a forwarder on such a
payment. The fixed-position theorems do not need the premise, because they spell out the route
from the settling sender's channel; for such payments they cover positions 1 to 6 of that
route. They are proved with T1, T2 and T3 in place, and we have not checked them without, so for
such payments bypass resistance at positions 1 to 6 rests on the liveness restrictions; only
Theorem~\ref{thm:anyhop} (single-offer payments) is shown to hold without them.

\emph{Weak conclusion.} All bypass theorems conclude only that some party's long-term key was
compromised in the trace: not which party (e.g., a neighbour of the bypassed
forwarder, as in the wormhole), and not that the compromise precedes the settlement or the
refund. Only Lemma~\ref{lem:skip} orders the compromise before the skip.

\subsection{Countermeasure: PTLC}\label{sec:ptlc}

The wormhole works because every channel of an HTLC route is locked with the same hash $h(x)$:
once $x$ is known at one point of the route, a compromised party can use it on a channel further
upstream. Malavolta et al.~\cite{malavolta2019} replace the shared lock by anonymous multi-hop
locks, the basis of point time-locked contracts (PTLCs): every channel has its own lock, and a
forwarder can compute the secret of its incoming lock only from the secret of its outgoing lock
and its own tweak, chosen by the sender. We compare both designs in two small models of
\code{lightning_aux.spthy}, \code{-D=PTLC} and the baseline \code{-D=HTLC}, that differ only in
how the channel secrets are derived (\code{Sender_Setup}) and in how an honest hop obtains its
incoming secret (\code{Hop_Settle}).

\paragraph{Model}
Both models fix the route S$\,\to\,$H1$\,\to\,$H2$\,\to\,$H3$\,\to\,$R over channels
\code{c0} to \code{c3}. \code{Sender_Setup} draws a fresh secret $x$ and per-hop tweaks
$t_1, t_2, t_3$ and derives the channel secrets by a hash chain
$s_{\mathit{in}} = h(\langle s_{\mathit{out}}, t\rangle)$: $s_3 = x$ and
$s_{i-1} = h(\langle s_i, t_i\rangle)$ for $i = 3, 2, 1$; channel $c_i$ is locked with
$h(s_i)$; an honest hop derives its incoming secret from its outgoing one with its tweak
(\code{Hop_Settle}; both rules in Appendix~\ref{lst:ptlc}). The hash chain is a
symbolic stand-in for the additive tweak of PTLCs built from adaptor
signatures~\cite{malavolta2019}, $s_{\mathit{in}} = s_{\mathit{out}} + t$: in both, deriving a
hop's incoming secret requires that hop's tweak. In the HTLC baseline,
$s_0 = s_1 = s_2 = s_3 = x$, i.e., one hash lock $h(x)$ on every channel, and \code{Hop_Settle}
passes the outgoing secret on unchanged. The adversary is the same in both models: Dolev-Yao
plus compromise of any party at any time (rule \code{Compromise}). A compromised hop leaks its
tweak (\code{Leak_Tweak}); every secret used to claim a channel is published, as it would be on
chain; a compromised payee may claim its channel with any value the adversary knows and that
opens the lock (\code{Adversary_Claim}). There is no skip rule: a wormhole has to be built from
adversary knowledge alone. Timing is abstracted: an open HTLC may be refunded at any time
(\code{Refund}).

\begin{lemma}[No Wormhole with Per-Hop Locks]
In the PTLC model, if S and H2 are never compromised, the sender's channel \texttt{c0} is
never redeemed while H2's outgoing channel \texttt{c2} is refunded, whichever of H1, H3 and R
are compromised ({\upshape\code{No_Wormhole_Around_Honest_Hop}}, all-traces, verified,
25~steps).
\end{lemma}
\noindent With the shared hash lock, the same situation is reachable
(\code{Wormhole_Around_Honest_Hop}, exists-trace, verified, 12 steps); both statements are
printed in Appendix~\ref{app:aux}. Two control lemmas show
that the PTLC result is not vacuous. \code{Control_Refund_Of_H2_Outgoing} (exists-trace,
6 steps): H2's outgoing HTLC can be refunded while H2 is honest, so the refund in the premise is
not excluded by the model itself. \code{Control_Wormhole_If_H2_Compromised} (exists-trace,
18 steps): once H2 itself is compromised, the bypass is reachable with S still uncompromised, so
the safety proof depends on H2 being honest. Sanity lemmas in both models show that a payment
completes end to end without any compromise (\code{Honest_Payment_Completes}: 10 steps in each) and with both neighbours H1 and H3 of H2 compromised
(\code{Colluders_Present_Payment_Completes}: 14 steps in PTLC, 8 in HTLC). All eight lemmas are
verified by \texttt{make aux} (PTLC 5/5, HTLC 3/3).

\paragraph{Limitations}
The two models are separate from \code{lightning.spthy}: a fixed route with three
intermediaries and one honest hop under attack, no channel layer, abstract timing, and an honest
\code{Sender_Setup} that chooses all locks and tweaks consistently; the safety lemma also
assumes that the sender is never compromised. The hash chain abstracts the group operation of
the adaptor-signature construction and does not model the algebra of~\cite{malavolta2019} (an
XOR encoding exhausted memory in \tamarin). We therefore claim the contrast between the two lock
designs on this route, not a PTLC result for routes of any length.
