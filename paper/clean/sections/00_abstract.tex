\begin{abstract}
The Lightning Network routes payments over chains of payment channels using
hash time-locked contracts (HTLCs). One \tamarin{} theory models its channel
layer (unboundedly many updates, revocation, punishment) and multi-hop routing
over routes of any length, against a Dolev-Yao adversary that can compromise
long-term keys. We prove that only cheating or compromised parties are
punished, that the receiver's invoice authenticates settlements unless the
receiver's key is compromised, and that preimages, which our model never sends,
stay secret. Under three liveness restrictions, which prune every violating
run, including runs in which an honest intermediary cannot act in time, an
uncompromised intermediary never has its outgoing HTLC redeemed while its
incoming one is refunded, and, without key compromise, payment atomicity holds
across any two consecutive intermediaries. Timely claiming is necessary: without
it, an intermediary can lose funds in a compromise-free run. In a
\tamarin-checked wormhole trace, colluders bypass an honest intermediary, which
is refunded but earns no fee. For routes of any length, a hop of a settled
payment whose hash is offered once is bypassed only if some long-term key is
compromised. On a fixed route with three intermediaries, per-hop locks as in
point time-locked contracts prevent bypassing the honest middle one when the
sender is honest; plain HTLCs admit it. All results are machine-checked with \tamarin~1.10.
\end{abstract}
