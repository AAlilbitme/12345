\subsection{Protocol Properties}\label{sec:properties}

We state the guarantees of \code{lightning.spthy} as lemmas, each named after the \tamarin{}
property that establishes it. We apply honesty assumptions explicitly, in each lemma, not
globally; only the restrictions of Sec.~\ref{sec:restrictions} hold in every trace. Unless
stated otherwise, every lemma is proved in the default mode (\texttt{make verify}), under all
nine restrictions, including T1 to T3. We sort all lemmas into ten groups, one per stage of a payment that they constrain
(Table~\ref{tab:groups} in Appendix~\ref{lst:groups}; the circled numbers in
Figs.~\ref{fig:inflight} and~\ref{fig:wormhole}): channel layer and keys~(0), invoice and
preimage~(1), lock~(2) and release~(3) causality, atomicity~(4), fees~(5), refund and
griefing~(6), wormhole~(7), intermediary safety and timing~(8), hop bypass~(9). We present the
central lemmas below; Appendix~\ref{app:catalogue} states every lemma verbatim.

The first guarantee is atomicity: on a route with two forwarders and no key compromise, once
the receiver has redeemed its HTLC, the sender can no longer be refunded. In general:

\begin{lemma}[Payment Atomicity]\label{lem:atomicity}
For any two consecutive forwarders $F_1$ and $F_2$ on a payment with hash $y$ (the incoming HTLC
of $F_2$ is the outgoing HTLC of $F_1$): if the outgoing HTLC of $F_2$ on $y$ is redeemed and no
party's key was ever compromised, then the incoming HTLC of $F_1$ on $y$ is never refunded, by
anyone (\code{Payment_Atomicity_Under_Liveness}, Appendix~\ref{app:atomicity}).
\end{lemma}

The second guarantee protects the intermediaries, who lock their own funds for a payment that
is not theirs.

\begin{lemma}[Intermediary Safety]\label{lem:intermediary}
If a forwarder's outgoing HTLC is redeemed while its incoming HTLC is refunded, then that
forwarder's long-term key was compromised (\code{Intermediary_Never_Loses_Under_Liveness},
Appendix~\ref{app:safety}).
\end{lemma}
Lemma~\ref{lem:intermediary} does not say when the key was compromised: a compromise after the
refund also satisfies it. The strong form closes this gap.

\begin{lemma}[Intermediary Safety, Strong Form]\label{lem:intermediary-strong}
Under the premises of Lemma~\ref{lem:intermediary}, that forwarder's key was compromised before
its incoming HTLC timed out (\code{Intermediary_Never_Loses_Strong}, Appendix~\ref{app:safety}).
\end{lemma}
This matches T2 and T3, which bind only forwarders not compromised before their incoming HTLC
times out. As \code{Forward_HTLC} is one generic rule, Lemmas~\ref{lem:atomicity}
to~\ref{lem:intermediary-strong} hold for every hop of a route of any length (under T1 to T3).
Routing causality says that nothing is forwarded out of thin air.

\begin{lemma}[Routing Causality]\label{lem:causality}
Every forward on hash $y$ is preceded by the HTLC it forwards: either the sender's offer on the
forwarder's incoming channel, or the predecessor's forward that locked that channel's HTLC
(\code{Forward_Requires_Incoming}, Appendix~\ref{app:lock}).
\end{lemma}
This one lemma covers every hop, where a model with a fixed path needs one lemma per position
(and an honest variant of each). It needs no honesty premise, because a forward consumes the
linear \code{Route} fact, which only \code{Sender_Offer_HTLC} and \code{Forward_HTLC} produce. On
the release side, every redemption stems from the receiver's fulfilment or from a forwarder
settling that hop (\code{Redeemed_Source}), and every preimage with which a forwarder or the
sender settles from an earlier redemption on that channel or an earlier wormhole skip
(\code{Fulfilled_Source}). Finally, a forwarder is paid only if its service succeeded.

\begin{lemma}[Fees Only on Success]\label{lem:fee}
A forwarder whose key is never compromised earns the fee of a hop only after it has forwarded
on that hop and its outgoing HTLC has been redeemed (\code{Fee_Only_On_Successful_Forward},
Appendix~\ref{app:fees}).
\end{lemma}
Per hop, the incoming amount is the outgoing amount plus the fee by construction, but amounts
are not chained along the route (Sec.~\ref{sec:core-amounts}): we make no end-to-end value claim.

\phantomsection\label{par:nonvacuity}%
\paragraph{Non-vacuity}
Safety lemmas are useless if the model cannot run at all, so we checked \code{exists-trace}
witnesses (Appendix~\ref{app:atomicity}): an HTLC is routed over two
forwarders to the receiver, who fulfils it (\code{Multihop_Routing_Reachable}); payments over
one and over two forwarders settle end to end, with no key compromise and no refund
(\code{Sanity_Honest_Payment_Completes}, \code{Sanity_Atomic_Payment_Completes}; the second
satisfies the premises of Lemma~\ref{lem:atomicity}); three channels among four different
parties open with no key compromise and no dispute (\code{Distinct_Parties_Configuration}). A
compromised forwarder can lose (\code{Sanity_Compromised_Intermediary_Can_Lose}), so the
premises of Lemmas~\ref{lem:intermediary} and~\ref{lem:intermediary-strong} are satisfiable.

\subsection{Timing and Liveness}\label{sec:timing}

Timing enters \code{lightning.spthy} only through T1 to T3 and the lemma \code{ForwardTimeGap}
derived from T2 (Sec.~\ref{sec:restrictions}); concrete block heights appear only in auxiliary
models of \code{lightning_aux.spthy}. With \code{-D=CLTV}, a positive CLTV delta (restriction
\code{DeltaPositive}) leaves every forwarder a non-empty claim window: a preimage revealed before the outgoing deadline is revealed before the
incoming one (\code{Claim_Window_Nonempty}), also over two staggered hops
(\code{Staggered_Path_Safe}). The four-rule model \code{-D=TIMEOUT} has no ordering restriction
at all. As a two-sided check, \tamarin{} finds the early-timeout race there
(\code{Early_Timeout_Race}, exists-trace, 6 steps: a forwarder's incoming HTLC times out and is
refunded, and its outgoing HTLC is redeemed after that timeout), whereas in
\code{lightning.spthy} \code{Timeout_Race_Blocked} (Appendix~\ref{app:safety}) excludes it for
every forwarder that is not compromised before its incoming timeout, even with any single one of
T1, T2 and T3 removed (Table~\ref{tab:necessity}).

We removed each liveness restriction in turn and re-ran the five lemmas that may depend on
them (Table~\ref{tab:necessity}). Without T3, \tamarin{} finds a counterexample with no key
compromise (\code{Necessity_IMC_Honest_Intermediary_Loses}, exists-trace, 28 steps,
Appendix~\ref{app:safety}): the receiver redeems the outgoing HTLC of an intermediary that never
claims its incoming HTLC, and the sender's HTLC to it is refunded. This honest intermediary
loses, which violates Lemmas~\ref{lem:intermediary} and~\ref{lem:intermediary-strong} (whose
direct proof attempts without T3 gave no result within 600\,s). T2 only orders the two
timeouts, and no ordering rule can force an action: the claim is exactly T3, which is therefore
load-bearing for intermediary safety. The ordering restrictions are not needed in this way: T1
is needed by none of the five lemmas, and T2 not for intermediary safety. From the cells
without a result we conclude nothing: we do not claim that \code{ForwardTimeGap} or
Lemma~\ref{lem:atomicity} needs T2, or that Lemma~\ref{lem:atomicity} needs T3.

\paragraph{Liveness as Restrictions}
Two of our three liveness assumptions, T2 and T3, are \tamarin{} restrictions of the form
\emph{if A happens, then B happens} (T1 only orders a redemption before a timeout); in T3, for
instance, B is the redemption of the forwarder's incoming HTLC. A restriction removes every
trace that violates it, including traces in which B is impossible rather than merely late, so a
lemma proved under T3 says nothing about runs in which an honest forwarder \emph{cannot} claim
in time, which is where real timing attacks live. Likewise, T2 removes every trace in which an
honest forwarder's incoming HTLC times out while its outgoing HTLC has not timed out earlier.
We make the scope of this assumption explicit.

\emph{Which results depend on it.} Removing T3 breaks intermediary safety (above);
Table~\ref{tab:necessity} shows which of the five liveness lemmas survive the removal of each
restriction. The 24 lemmas of \texttt{make general} (the bypass theorem for routes of any length,
Sec.~\ref{sec:bypass-any}, its helpers and 16 lemmas of \texttt{make verify}, among them
\code{Skipped_Requires_Compromise} and \code{Refund_Requires_Timeout}) are proved with all three
liveness restrictions removed. The wormhole trace \code{Wormhole_Fee_Theft_Reachable} is verified with every restriction in place,
so the restrictions do not hide it. All other all-traces lemmas of \texttt{make verify},
including the bypass theorems for positions 1 to 6, are proved with all three restrictions in
place; apart from the five lemmas of Table~\ref{tab:necessity}, we have not checked which of
them still hold without them.

\emph{A claim window exists, in a separate model.} The Clock model (\code{-D=CLOCK}) gives
channels concrete deadlines on a block clock; nine lemmas (six all-traces, three exists-trace)
cover the channel and HTLC lifecycle, e.g., \code{No_HTLC_After_Close}. It has no liveness
restriction and proves \code{Claim_Window_Exists}: if a forwarder's outgoing HTLC is redeemed
and its incoming HTLC is refunded, the redemption happens at a strictly lower block height than
the refund, so the forwarder had a non-empty window in which to claim. It also proves
\code{Loss_Implies_Skipped_Claim}: a forwarder that loses in this way never claimed its incoming
HTLC. The Clock model is not composed with \code{lightning.spthy}, so it supports T3 but does
not discharge it.

\emph{Alternatives.} A more faithful treatment would replace the restrictions by the progress
semantics of Backes et al.~\cite{backes2017}: roughly, honest processes that can continue
without input from the adversary eventually do, so traces in which the forwarder cannot act are
kept, not pruned. \tamarin{} 1.10 implements it for \sapic{} processes (option
\code{translation-progress}), into which the routing layer would have to be rewritten. A cheaper
first step is to make T1, T2 and T3 premises of only the lemmas that need them, so that they no
longer prune traces for every other lemma. We leave both to future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Griefing and the Wormhole}\label{sec:wormhole}

\code{Refund_Possible} shows a griefing trace: the sender offers an HTLC against an invoice,
nobody forwards it, and it is refunded, so the payment never settles; no key is compromised.
Since a refund requires a timeout (\code{Refund_Requires_Timeout}), the capital stays locked
until then. This witness refunds the sender, not an intermediary. We report it as reachable
griefing (denial of service), not as a new attack (group 6, the circled 6 in
Fig.~\ref{fig:griefing}).

The wormhole attack~\cite{malavolta2019} is an instance of the agent-skipping attack formalised
symbolically by~\cite{smith2022}. It is a known attack, so we do not claim it as new. First,
after the linear single-spend fix (Sec.~\ref{par:single-spend}), it is a soundness check: the
model must still admit this behaviour, or the fix would be too strong. Second, the model shows
who can mount the attack: only two compromised parties on the same payment, through
\code{AttackerSkip} (Sec.~\ref{sec:core-skip}). \code{Wormhole_Fee_Theft_Reachable}
(exists-trace, verified by replaying a saved 116-step proof; Appendix~\ref{app:wormhole}) is the
five-party trace of Fig.~\ref{fig:wormhole} (circled 7). The HTLC on $y = h(x)$, invoiced by
Bob, is locked hop by hop from Alice through Att1, Indiana and Att2 to Bob. Bob redeems the HTLC
of Att2 with $x$ and is paid; \code{AttackerSkip} relays $x$ from Att2 to Att1, off the path, and
Att1 claims the HTLC of Alice, so Alice settles, while Indiana's outgoing HTLC is refunded on
timeout and Indiana earns no fee. The lemma requires that Alice, Bob and Indiana are never
compromised; Att1 and Att2 are, as \code{AttackerSkip} requires. An honest hop is thus bypassed although the
payment settles. Conversely, a skip requires a key compromise:

\begin{lemma}[Skips Require Compromise]\label{lem:skip}
Every wormhole skip happens after some party's long-term key was compromised
(\code{Skipped_Requires_Compromise}, Appendix~\ref{lst:skipped}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent Lemma~\ref{lem:skip} does not exclude the wormhole; it says that the wormhole needs a
compromise.
