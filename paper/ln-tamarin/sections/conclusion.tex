\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

We have given a modular, machine-checked model integrating the Lightning
two-party channel lifecycle with multi-hop HTLC routing and fees, establishing
61 distinct properties across five theories. The integration surfaced a linear
single-spend discipline: two independent soundness gaps arising from encoding
one-shot on-chain outputs as persistent facts, repaired uniformly by linear
tokens, and generalisable into an auditable modelling invariant. We established
the minimality of the T3 claim obligation empirically, proved value
conservation with strictly positive per-hop fees, exhibited a griefing denial
of service, reproduced the wormhole attack and quantified its theft as exactly
the bypassed fees, and mapped where the prover stops scaling.

Three directions follow. The refinement theorem should be machine-checked
rather than argued on paper. The concrete and abstract layers should be
reconciled so that message-layer attacks such as the wormhole can be stated at
unbounded path length. And the channel layer would benefit from the numeric
timing that currently lives only in the separate clock theories --- which, on
the evidence of \Cref{sec:discipline}, may become tractable once the model is
sufficiently linear.
