\section{Formal Modelling Methodology}

\subsection{The Curse of an All-in-One Theory}

Our initial approach was to put everything into a single theory: the channel
lifecycle, the HTLC routing logic, the CLTV block-height arithmetic, and value
conservation. It did not work. When the signature equational theory,
natural-number induction, and an unbounded block clock are combined,
\mtamarin's search space explodes and the prover exhausts available memory.

This was tested rather than assumed. Any \emph{two} of the three ingredients
remain tractable; only the full triple OOM-kills the prover. The model is
therefore split so that no single theory carries all three. The resulting
architecture is summarised in \Cref{tab:theories}.

\subsection{Tactic Engineering and Reproduction}

All results were produced under a strict test-first discipline: a property is
claimed only after \mtamarin\ reports it. Per-lemma invocation is used on the
large theory to avoid OOM. The flag \code{-{}-stop-on-trace=seqdfs} rescues the
heavy existence witnesses but hangs the natural-number arithmetic theories, so
it is applied selectively. All results were reproduced in a single sitting on
one machine under \mtamarin~1.10.0 with Maude~3.1; every theory passes
\mtamarin's well-formedness checks, and the whole package verifies in
$542.36$\,s of wall-clock time.
