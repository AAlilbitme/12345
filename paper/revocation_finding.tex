\subsection{A recurring pitfall: one-shot on-chain resources modeled as
persistent facts}
\label{sec:singlespend}

A symbolic model of an off-chain protocol must decide how to represent
\emph{on-chain} resources that can be spent exactly once---a funding output, an
HTLC output, a revocable commitment. Modeling such a resource as a Tamarin
\emph{persistent} fact (prefixed \texttt{!}, never consumed) is convenient but
unsound: a persistent fact can license the same rule to fire unboundedly, i.e.\
the ``output'' can be spent more than once. We found this pitfall twice, in two
different layers, and in each case the fix is the same: replace the persistent
fact with a \emph{linear} single-spend token, produced once and consumed on use.

\paragraph{Instance 1 --- the HTLC output (confirmed).}
The forwarding rules were gated only by a public offer message; nothing consumed
a per-output resource, so a replayed offer could re-fire an honest forward and
lock a second HTLC on the same channel output with an adversary-chosen amount
(machine-checked reachable). Replacing the implicit persistent permission with a
linear \texttt{Free(ptr)} slot---emitted once per channel open, consumed by each
HTLC-add---makes ``at most one HTLC per output'' a structural consequence; the
double-forward probe is then falsified with no axiom, and all lemmas still
verify.

\paragraph{Instance 2 --- the revoked commitment (analytical; re-verification
pending).}
The punishment rules \texttt{Publish\_Revoked\_A/B} were gated only by
persistent facts (\texttt{!RevokedSecret}, \texttt{!ValidOnChain}). A revoked
commitment transaction spends the funding output once on-chain, and the
counterparty's penalty claim spends the resulting output once; yet the
persistent gating lets \texttt{Publish\_Revoked}---and hence \texttt{Punish}%
---fire arbitrarily many times for a single revoked state. The fix mirrors
Instance~1: \texttt{Revoke\_Old\_Secret} emits a linear
\texttt{RevokedCommitmentUnspent} token (one per revoked commitment), consumed
by \texttt{Publish\_Revoked}, so the revoked output is spent at most once.
Persistent \emph{knowledge} of the revocation secret is retained
(\texttt{!RevokedSecret}); only the one-shot \emph{spend} is made linear.

By a monotonicity argument---adding an input fact only restricts a rule's
firings---every all-traces safety property is preserved and no existence witness
routes through the double-publish path, so we expect all $43$ lemmas to remain
verified and the probe \texttt{AUDIT\_Double\_Publish\_Revoked} to become
falsified. (This instance is stated as analytically sound pending machine
re-verification.)

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The two instances are not coincidental: they are the same category error in two
sublayers, and the linear-token remedy is uniform. We suggest that
\emph{``every one-shot on-chain output is a linear fact''} be treated as a
modeling invariant for symbolic PCN analyses, and that persistent facts be
reserved for genuinely non-consumable knowledge (keys, revealed secrets,
channel identity). This turns an easy-to-miss soundness gap into a checkable
discipline.
